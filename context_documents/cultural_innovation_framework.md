# Cultural Innovation Framework (Douglas Holt)

This document provides the cultural reasoning framework
used to analyze brands, technologies, and cultural phenomena.

It is intended as a reference for structured cultural analysis,
not as a teaching text.

---

## 1. Cultural Tension

Cultural tension refers to unresolved anxieties, contradictions,
or identity conflicts produced by large-scale social change.

These tensions typically emerge when:
- Economic conditions shift faster than social norms
- Technology reshapes work, relationships, or identity
- Established life narratives stop delivering dignity or security

Key questions:
- What feels broken, unstable, or contradictory in everyday life?
- What identities are under strain or losing coherence?
- What problems cannot be solved by existing institutions?

---

## 2. Historical & Social Context

Cultural tensions must be grounded in historical forces.

Relevant forces include:
- Economic cycles (growth, recession, precarity)
- Labor market transformation
- Technological disruption
- Political or ideological erosion
- Demographic or cultural shifts

Key questions:
- What historical conditions produced this tension?
- Why is this tension emerging now, not earlier?
- Which social groups are most affected?

---

## 3. Ideological Vacuum

An ideological vacuum appears when dominant belief systems
no longer help people make sense of their lives.

This occurs when:
- Traditional success narratives collapse
- Institutions lose credibility
- Moral or cultural authorities feel disconnected from reality

Key questions:
- Which ideologies are failing?
- What promises no longer feel believable?
- What guidance are people missing?

---

## 4. Ideological Myth (Cultural Solution)

An ideological myth is a symbolic narrative
that helps people imagine a way forward.

It does not solve material problems directly,
but offers emotional, moral, or identity resolution.

Effective myths:
- Reframe failure, struggle, or uncertainty
- Restore dignity or agency
- Offer a coherent identity people can inhabit

Key questions:
- What story resolves the tension symbolically?
- What kind of person does this myth celebrate?
- What values are restored or redefined?

---

## 5. Cultural Authority

Cultural authority legitimizes the myth.

Authority can come from:
- Subcultures
- Marginalized groups
- Historical traditions
- Countercultural movements
- Artistic or intellectual communities

Key questions:
- Who has the credibility to tell this story?
- Why should people trust this myth?
- What cultural sources make it feel authentic?

---

## 6. Rituals & Symbols

Rituals allow people to enact the ideology in daily life.

They transform abstract meaning into lived practice.

Examples:
- Repeated behaviors
- Symbolic objects
- Language, gestures, or habits
- Participation in shared moments

Key questions:
- How do people perform this ideology?
- What symbols make it tangible?
- How is meaning repeated and reinforced?

---

## Usage Notes

- Always analyze all six components.
- Do not skip ideological vacuum.
- Avoid feature-based or marketing-driven explanations.
- Focus on meaning, identity, and cultural logic.
