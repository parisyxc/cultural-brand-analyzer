# Cultural Archetype Router

This document defines a routing logic
that helps identify which cultural archetype
a new brand, product, technology, or phenomenon
most closely aligns with.

The router does not explain archetypes.
It guides analytical judgment.

---

## Purpose

Before deep analysis, determine:
Which cultural innovation archetype
best frames the case at hand.

Correct routing ensures:
- Appropriate analytical focus
- Relevant cultural comparisons
- Accurate interpretation of meaning creation

---

## Routing Principles

Always route based on:
- Primary cultural function (not category)
- Source of meaning (ideology, symbol, organization, ritual)
- How cultural tension is resolved

Do NOT route based on:
- Industry
- Product features
- Marketing tactics
- Brand size or age

---

## Archetype Overview

### 1. Ideological Innovation
**(Reference: Nike)**

**Core function:**
Rewrites dominant success or identity narratives.

**Primary signal:**
People use the brand to reinterpret struggle, effort, or self-worth.

**Key question:**
Does this brand offer a new moral story about who deserves dignity?

---

### 2. Mythic Corporation
**(Reference: Jack Daniel's)**

**Core function:**
The organization itself embodies a cultural myth.

**Primary signal:**
Authenticity comes from unchanged practices, origin stories, and restraint.

**Key question:**
Is meaning produced by how the company exists, not what it says?

---

### 3. Ideological Hotspot
**(Reference: Ben & Jerry's)**

**Core function:**
Becomes a platform for moral and political expression.

**Primary signal:**
The brand is treated as a social actor, not a neutral business.

**Key question:**
Has the brand become a focal point for unresolved societal debates?

---

### 4. Cultural Codes & Symbolic Compression
**(Reference: Marlboro)**

**Core function:**
Encodes identity into simple, repeatable symbols.

**Primary signal:**
Meaning is instantly recognized without explanation.

**Key question:**
Does the brand rely on repetition of symbols rather than narrative?

---

### 5. Cultural Capital Commodification
**(Reference: Starbucks)**

**Core function:**
Teaches taste, sophistication, or status through ritual.

**Primary signal:**
Consumption functions as cultural self-upgrading.

**Key question:**
Does the brand democratize elite cultural knowledge or practice?

---

### 6. Activism as Lifestyle
**(Reference: Patagonia)**

**Core function:**
Turns moral concern into repeatable everyday practice.

**Primary signal:**
Values are enacted through behavior, not slogans.

**Key question:**
Does the brand make ethical living feel achievable and durable?

---

### 7. Product as Myth
**(Reference: Vitaminwater)**

**Core function:**
The product itself resolves cultural anxiety symbolically.

**Primary signal:**
Design and ritual matter more than functional superiority.

**Key question:**
Is the product experienced as a completed moral or identity act?

---

## Routing Decision Process

When analyzing a new case:

1. Identify the dominant cultural tension.
2. Determine how meaning is primarily created:
   - Narrative?
   - Organization?
   - Symbol?
   - Ritual?
   - Product form?
3. Match the case to the archetype
   whose core function best resolves that tension.

If multiple archetypes appear relevant:
- Select the dominant one
- Note secondary influences, but do not merge categories

---

## Non-Negotiable Rule

Always route before deep analysis.

Misrouting leads to:
- Feature-level explanations
- Superficial insight
- Misdiagnosis of cultural power

Correct routing precedes correct thinking.
